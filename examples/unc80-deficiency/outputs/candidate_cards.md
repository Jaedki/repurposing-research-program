## ganaxolone

Ganaxolone is a scientifically testable symptomatic rescue for the seizure-positive subset of UNC80 deficiency because it positively modulates GABA-A receptors and potentiates GABA-evoked chloride currents (PMID:9067315); in native mouse hippocampal neurons it preferentially enhanced extrasynaptic δ-subunit-mediated tonic currents, and loss of that subunit weakened both the current response and protection in a kindling model (PMID:29602830). The proposed directional bridge is therefore increased phasic and tonic inhibitory restraint leading to fewer recurrent network bursts, but it is conditional rather than a correction of the UNC80 lesion: UNC80 loss reduces a depolarizing NALCN sodium-leak current, while seizures are incompletely penetrant and can be absent despite severe encephalopathy (PMID:32620897; PMCID:PMC6671679; PMCID:PMC4716667), so inhibitory-neuron dysfunction, developmental compensation, or compartment-specific channelosome defects could instead dominate, and ganaxolone could be ineffective or paradoxical if GABA is not hyperpolarizing in the relevant cells. Translation is supported across other epilepsies: a phase 3 trial in CDKL5 deficiency disorder reduced median 28-day major motor seizure frequency by 30.7% versus 6.9% with placebo (p=0.0036; PMID:35429480), whereas a 21-participant PCDH19 trial showed a numerically larger reduction that did not reach significance (p=0.17; PMID:36870093), demonstrating that benefit is neither uniform nor automatically transferable between genetic encephalopathies. Oral ganaxolone is rapidly absorbed with approximately dose-proportional systemic exposure in healthy volunteers (PMID:9579942), but no UNC80-specific brain exposure, pharmacodynamic target engagement, or exposure-response data exist, and somnolence is a recurrent adverse effect (PMID:35429480; PMID:36870093; PMID:9579942). No published or registered exact-disease ganaxolone use or trial was identified, so the central evidence remains a cross-disease inference; the receptor and kindling assays also used non-UNC80 mouse preparations and voltage-clamp or behavioral endpoints that do not establish hyperpolarizing GABA or circuit rescue in UNC80 networks, while sedation could distort motor-seizure observation unless electrographic activity is measured. A decisive test should therefore compare vehicle and clinically relevant free ganaxolone exposures in genotype-matched UNC80 excitatory and inhibitory neuronal networks while jointly measuring GABA reversal potential, tonic and phasic currents, baseline firing, spontaneous electrographic bursts, and sedation-independent network readouts, with failure to potentiate hyperpolarizing current or reduce bursts falsifying the hypothesis.

## Capsaicin

Capsaicin provides a biologically coherent but highly conditional event-level hypothesis for recurrent aspiration in UNC80 deficiency: the disease literature establishes recurrent aspiration in one biallelic UNC80 case and severe aspiration pneumonia in another, but it does not identify delayed pharyngeal triggering, deficient laryngeal sensation, or delayed laryngeal-vestibule closure as the operative lesion (DOI:10.1016/j.ajhg.2015.11.013; DOI:10.7759/cureus.97861). Local oropharyngeal capsaicin is a TRPV1 sensory agonist, and the proposed directional bridge is that stronger afferent input, with associated substance-P signalling and sensory-network activation, could accelerate laryngeal-vestibule closure and thereby reduce penetration or aspiration during exposed swallows. The bridge has human action evidence in older adults with oropharyngeal dysphagia: a 2019 pilot found no acute improvement but, after 10 treatment days, reported a 22.5% shorter closure time and a 24.2% lower penetration-aspiration score than placebo (PMID:31068978), and a newer double-blind trial using 10 mL of 10 μM capsaicin before meals three times daily for two weeks reported lower PAS, shorter closure time, shorter pharyngeal sensory-evoked-potential latency, and a 75.8% increase in salivary substance P, whereas placebo did not improve these measures (PMID:41147275). A separate crossover study also observed increased salivary substance P and shorter cervical oesophageal opening only in the capsaicin-responsive subgroup, so that association does not by itself establish substance P as the causal mediator and may partly reflect response-defined subgrouping (PMID:28319947). Across small trials in elderly or post-stroke populations, pooled swallowing outcomes favour capsaicin, but the review identified few studies, unclear bias, heterogeneous delivery modes and diseases, and predominantly surrogate or short-term swallowing endpoints rather than pneumonia prevention (DOI:10.3389/fnagi.2022.931016). An infant-pig sensory-lesion experiment adds developmental plausibility but also mechanism specificity: capsaicin changed feeding physiology and restored only some variables, showing that sensory stimulation does not generically reverse dysphagia (PMID:40641302). The central uncertainty is therefore not target engagement but lesion matching: UNC80 aspiration could instead arise from oral-motor discoordination, generalized hypotonia and ineffective cough, post-swallow residue, reflux, seizures or impaired alertness, and none of these would necessarily be corrected by pharyngeal TRPV1 stimulation. The tested exposure is repeated local oral dosing in older adults; efficacy, burning or cough tolerability, dosing, aspiration risk during administration, and feasibility in severely neurologically impaired children or people whose feeding bypasses the oropharynx remain unknown. No exact-disease capsaicin use or testing was identified in the packet-scoped literature search. A discriminating experiment would first phenotype swallow phase and sensory status, then compare vehicle with a locally delivered tolerated dose using videofluoroscopic laryngeal-closure latency and PAS, while separately tracking residue, cough/clearance, respiratory events, and adverse responses; benefit confined to sensory-delay phenotypes would support the proposed bridge, whereas unchanged closure timing or worsening airway invasion would refute it.

## Prucalopride

Prucalopride is a potent 5-HT4-receptor agonist whose receptor-panel profile showed high affinity at human 5-HT4 isoforms and at least 290-fold selectivity over the measured off-targets, supporting the asserted action rather than a nonspecific prokinetic label (DOI:10.1016/s0014-2999(01)01087-1). The proposed bridge is therefore directionally coherent but compensatory: enhancing recruitable enteric cholinergic and gastric motor output could accelerate antral-pyloric transit without repairing UNC80 or the NALCN channelosome, and it would fail if the limiting lesion is mechanical obstruction, nonrecruitable gastric muscle or pacemaker circuitry, impaired pyloric coordination, or a symptom source outside gastric emptying. Disease relevance remains provisional because delayed gastric emptying was asserted, not objectively measured, in a single child with a clinical UNC80-disease label during a setting complicated by profound hypotonia, gastrostomy feeding, severe constipation, infection, vomiting, dehydration and medication exposure (DOI:10.7759/cureus.4395), while the UNC80-to-NALCN-to-gastric-motility link is extrapolated from reduced NALCN current for one UNC80 variant in heterologous cells (DOI:10.1016/j.ajhg.2015.11.004) and NALCN-dependent substance-P modulation of neonatal mouse small-intestinal interstitial cells of Cajal in which basal pacemaking persisted (DOI:10.1159/000338504). Empirical translation is encouraging but heterogeneous: a short placebo-controlled adult crossover study in predominantly idiopathic gastroparesis found both faster gastric half-emptying and improved symptoms at 2 mg daily, albeit with nausea/headache withdrawals and one small-bowel volvulus (DOI:10.14309/ajg.0000000000000304), whereas a 15-person diabetic/connective-tissue-disease crossover pilot found faster scintigraphic emptying at 4 mg daily without improvement in gastroparesis or meal-related symptoms (DOI:10.1111/nmo.13958), and a randomized healthy-volunteer study found no gastric-emptying effect despite accelerated colonic transit (DOI:10.1136/gut.44.5.682); these nulls show that target engagement and faster transit are context dependent and that emptying is not a reliable surrogate for symptom benefit. Pediatric exposure is documented but not disease-specific: one objectively diagnosed 8-year-old improved symptomatically and on repeat scintigraphy during 0.03-0.04 mg/kg/day treatment (DOI:10.5546/aap.2022.eng.e98), and a single-centre retrospective, open-label series using up to 0.04 mg/kg/day reported improvement in 65% of 71 young people with refractory upper-gastrointestinal symptoms, including apparent enrichment among enterally fed or neurologically comorbid patients (DOI:10.1007/s40272-021-00489-5), but the case report and uncontrolled chart review are vulnerable to regression to the mean, co-interventions, selective follow-up and subjective ascertainment, and most participants were much older than the focal child. Conversely, a 213-child placebo-controlled trial at the same weight-based dose found no benefit for functional constipation, a distinct distal endpoint that does not refute the gastric hypothesis but cautions against assuming generalized pediatric dysmotility responsiveness (DOI:10.1053/j.gastro.2014.09.005). No exact-disease use or testing of prucalopride in molecularly confirmed UNC80 deficiency was located; viability therefore depends on first confirming persistent delayed gastric emptying with an age-appropriate objective study, excluding obstruction and reversible confounders, demonstrating that antral-pyloric circuitry is pharmacologically recruitable at tolerable pediatric exposure, and prospectively separating emptying, symptom, feeding-tolerance, reflux, constipation and safety outcomes.

## perampanel

Perampanel is a selective, noncompetitive negative allosteric AMPA-receptor antagonist that concentration-dependently suppresses AMPA- and kainate-evoked currents in cultured hippocampal neurons, making reduced fast glutamatergic recruitment a pharmacologically coherent way to limit recurrent seizure propagation (PMID:25229608). In UNC80 deficiency, however, seizures are variably penetrant and coexist with a proximal reduction of UNC80-dependent NALCN sodium-leak current and variant-dependent dendritic channelosome mislocalization, so the directionally favorable step from AMPA blockade to less ictal spread is an inference about a downstream network dependency, not restoration of the causal channel defect; excessive AMPA suppression could instead further reduce baseline activity in already hypoexcitable cells (PMID:32620897; PMID:26708751; PMCID:PMC6671679; PMCID:PMC4716667). Human translation is supported only across other epilepsies: in an industry-funded randomized trial of idiopathic generalized tonic-clonic seizures, adjunctive 2-8 mg/day perampanel increased responder and seizure-freedom rates versus placebo, but most participants were not seizure-free and dizziness and fatigue were common (PMID:26296511), while a heterogeneous real-world drug-resistant cohort found 29% with no seizure reduction, 9% with paradoxical aggravation, frequent psychiatric, dizziness, and sleepiness adverse effects, and 41% discontinuation, with the retrospective design, polytherapy, titration differences, and confounding by indication limiting mechanistic interpretation (PMID:33940525). Observed plasma concentrations overlap numerically with the submicromolar in-vitro inhibitory range, yet total plasma concentration cannot be equated to unbound brain exposure or receptor occupancy, and a prospective observational study found no significant concentration-response or concentration-tolerability relationship (PMID:32858369). The chief rival explanation is therefore ordinary broad-spectrum symptomatic seizure suppression unrelated to UNC80 biology; the critical test is a genotype-matched excitatory/inhibitory neuronal-network assay showing reduced burst recruitment or duration without further loss of baseline firing, followed by exposure-anchored evaluation in the relevant developmental age group. No perampanel-specific use, trial, or experimental test in genetically confirmed UNC80 deficiency was identified, and the available exact-disease cohort evidence establishes variable, sometimes refractory epilepsy without resolving which circuits are AMPA-dependent (PMID:40048676).

## suvorexant

Suvorexant is an orally administered dual OX1R/OX2R antagonist that suppresses wake-promoting orexin signalling, so its plausible role in UNC80 deficiency is downstream sleep-state stabilization rather than correction of UNC80 or NALCN-channel-complex function (DAILYMED:e5b72731-1acb-45b7-9c13-290ad12d3951). The directional bridge is explicitly conditional: if the shortened early sleep bouts and excess transitions measured in unc-80 loss-of-function worms transfer to a human orexin-dependent failure of sleep maintenance, attenuating orexin drive could lengthen bouts and reduce early-night wakefulness. Two unc-80 alleles support the worm phenotype, but the readout is whole-animal quiescence in liquid, delayed excess quiescence is only inferred to be rebound, and an earlier assay found normal total sleep with a distinct sleep-bout arousal abnormality, making the phenotype assay- and context-dependent (PMCID:PMC13334172; PMCID:PMC6283151). Adult insomnia trials provide empirical action-level support: suvorexant improves sleep maintenance and onset, and the pivotal trials found no marked withdrawal or rebound signal on discontinuation (PMCID:PMC3542986; DOI:10.1016/j.biopsych.2014.10.003). Translation is nevertheless uncertain: human UNC80 reports contain heterogeneous sleep complaints without bout-resolved polysomnography, actigraphy, circadian phase measurements, or evidence that orexin drive is excessive, so circadian-output failure, obstructive or other respiratory sleep disruption, seizures, concomitant CNS depressants, and caregiving schedules remain rival explanations (PMCID:PMC4716670; DOI:10.1371/journal.pone.0078147). A four-patient paediatric neurodevelopmental-disorder case series showed one robust response, one response only with trazodone, and two mild or absent responses, supporting feasibility but not predictable efficacy (DOI:10.1007/s00787-021-01883-7). Respiratory reassurance is limited: at 40 mg in mild-to-moderate obstructive sleep apnoea, mean oxygen saturation was unchanged, but apnea-hypopnea index rose slightly and respiratory effects varied between and within individuals; severe apnoea was not studied (DOI:10.5664/jcsm.5382; DAILYMED:e5b72731-1acb-45b7-9c13-290ad12d3951). Exposure is clinically material because the label reports an approximately 12-hour half-life, CYP3A-mediated metabolism, additive CNS depression, next-day somnolence and motor impairment, and no established paediatric safety or efficacy; these issues are especially relevant to severe hypotonia, immobility, antiseizure polypharmacy, and possible respiratory disease (DAILYMED:e5b72731-1acb-45b7-9c13-290ad12d3951). No exact-disease suvorexant report or trial was located, and current UNC80 guidance states that no disease-specific practice guideline exists while noting benefit only from melatonin and risperidone for sleep difficulties (NCBI-BOOKSHELF:NBK453434). A discriminating experiment would therefore require baseline and on-treatment bout-resolved polysomnography or validated actigraphy, respiratory and circadian characterization, and demonstration of fewer early-night transitions without worsened delayed hypersomnolence, rebound-like quiescence, respiratory indices, or daytime function.

## Metoclopramide

Metoclopramide is a testable compensatory prokinetic hypothesis, not a correction of UNC80 or NALCN: its established action increases upper-gastrointestinal motility by increasing especially antral contraction tone and amplitude and relaxing the pylorus (https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=3fb9a45e-48ba-45c5-86a7-03a8744888f6), so if the reported delayed gastric emptying in UNC80 deficiency reflects a recruitable antral-pyloric motor apparatus downstream of impaired neuromodulation, the directional prediction is faster objectively measured emptying. That bridge is inferential; the disease observation is an unquantified attribution in one child during a context that included severe hypotonia, constipation, tube feeding, enterovirus infection, pneumonia, dehydration and vomiting (DOI:10.7759/cureus.4395), while the proposed NALCN-interstitial-cell-of-Cajal connection comes from murine small-intestinal cells in which NALCN loss reduced substance-P-evoked modulation but spared basal pacemaking (DOI:10.1159/000338504), and no evidence shows metoclopramide restores UNC80-NALCN current or has been tested in UNC80-deficient gastric tissue. Human pediatric translation is biologically encouraging but heterogeneous: in a small randomized intravenous study metoclopramide more than doubled gastric emptying in infants with regurgitation or postsurgical gastroparesis but had no significant effect in prematurity-associated gastroparesis (PMID:3351700), arguing that a response cannot be assumed across rival causes such as infection, mechanical obstruction, medication effects, severe constipation, oral-motor dysfunction or generalized hypotonia. No exact-disease report or trial of metoclopramide was identified; the current UNC80 GeneReview describes supportive feeding or gastrostomy care and standard constipation management rather than a gastric-emptying intervention (NCBI-BOOKSHELF:NBK453434). Exposure and safety are major translation constraints: the cited U.S. label is adult-only for this formulation, says pediatric safety and effectiveness are unestablished, contraindicates use in epilepsy and when motility stimulation would be dangerous, and warns of acute extrapyramidal reactions and potentially irreversible tardive dyskinesia (https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=3fb9a45e-48ba-45c5-86a7-03a8744888f6); a pediatric systematic review estimated extrapyramidal symptoms in 9% and sedation in 6% of prospective multiple-dose studies, with heterogeneous definitions and mostly moderate risk of bias (DOI:10.1007/s40264-016-0418-9). Because dyskinesia and seizures are common in UNC80 deficiency, central adverse effects could be both especially consequential and difficult to distinguish from baseline disease. Viability therefore turns on first confirming delayed gastric emptying and excluding obstruction, then showing preserved antral-pyloric responsiveness and drug exposure with a gastric-emptying endpoint that separates prokinesis from antiemesis; durability, genotype dependence, dose-route pharmacokinetics and neurologic tolerability in this population remain unknown.

## Erythromycin A

Erythromycin A is a plausible acute compensatory probe for the reported delayed gastric emptying in UNC80 deficiency because it directly activates the human motilin receptor and, in isolated human antrum, erythromycin lactobionate facilitates nerve-evoked cholinergic contractions [DOI:10.1111/bph.12077], while controlled human manometry shows increased antral contraction amplitude and improved antroduodenal coordination after erythromycin [DOI:10.1016/0016-5085(92)90164-t]. The disease bridge is explicitly inferential: the only UNC80 report asserting delayed emptying did not objectively measure gastric transit or exclude outlet obstruction [DOI:10.7759/cureus.4395], and the proposed reduced UNC80-NALCN current and blunted neuropeptide modulation of interstitial-cell-of-Cajal activity come from separate heterologous-cell and neonatal-mouse intestinal experiments, with basal pacemaking preserved after Nalcn loss [DOI:10.1016/j.ajhg.2015.11.004; DOI:10.1159/000338504]. Thus erythromycin would not repair UNC80 or NALCN; it would test whether a residual motilin-responsive antral and gastroduodenal motor apparatus can be recruited strongly enough to shorten objectively measured emptying. That residual-capacity requirement is material: intravenous erythromycin induced phase III activity in 18 of 20 children who already displayed fasting phase III but in only one of 15 who did not, and the higher paediatric dose produced substantially more pain, nausea, or vomiting [DOI:10.1007/BF02088040]. Translation from a motility readout to benefit is also uncertain, because a randomized crossover study in patients with delayed emptying approximately halved solid emptying time yet did not improve cumulative meal-related symptom severity [DOI:10.1136/gut.2003.035279]. Receptor desensitization is biologically possible, although erythromycin A was much less desensitizing than ABT-229 and did not measurably internalize the receptor in the reported CHO-MTLR assay [DOI:10.1124/jpet.104.081497]; chronic oral exposure, erratic absorption during gastric stasis, formulation differences between erythromycin A and administered salts, antimicrobial selection pressure, and gastrointestinal intolerance therefore remain practical limitations. Cardiac repolarization and interaction risk are nontrivial: current oral erythromycin use was associated with increased sudden cardiac death in an observational cohort, with a larger association during concurrent strong CYP3A inhibition [DOI:10.1056/NEJMoa040582]. Targeted searches found no indexed report of erythromycin use or testing in molecularly confirmed UNC80 deficiency. Before any translational interpretation, the focal phenotype needs objective solid- and liquid-emptying measurement, exclusion of mechanical and pyloric disease, separation from constipation, reflux, tube-feeding regimen, infection, medication effects, and generalized hypotonia, and a meal-linked dose-response study must demonstrate coordinated antral-duodenal propulsion rather than painful nonpropagating contractions; absence of a motor or emptying response would falsify this compensatory route.

## D-mannitol

Inhaled D-mannitol is a biologically coherent but untested compartment-level compensation for secretion retention in UNC80 deficiency: its osmotic action draws water into the airway lumen, and human radioaerosol studies show increased mucociliary clearance, so the directional inference is that better airway-surface hydration and less adherent mucus could move more material proximally before the child's deficient inspiratory, glottic, and expiratory cough phases become limiting (PMID:23362816; PMID:9426077). The disease anchor is much weaker than the pharmacology: one three-year-old child was reported to need cough assist four times daily and to be unable to clear secretions because of hypotonia, but no cough-flow, respiratory-pressure, mucus-rheology, ciliary, or aspiration measurements were made, the child carried broad homozygous genomic regions that permit a modifier explanation, and a separate biallelic-UNC80 cohort recorded no breathing abnormalities in 0/12 despite frequent infections, making both phenotype prevalence and UNC80-specific attribution uncertain (DOI:10.7759/cureus.4395; PMCID:PMC6671679). Translation outside UNC80 provides a useful positive signal but also exposes a major artefact: in seven stable adults with asthma and mucociliary dysfunction, 240 or 480 mg inhaled mannitol increased 60-minute clearance from 5.5% at baseline to 19.5% or 26.4%, yet every participant received eformoterol and the protocol added 100 coached coughs, so the result does not establish effective secretion extraction in a profoundly hypotonic child with weak cough (PMID:20576419). The current product label likewise confirms that pulmonary delivery depends on the patient's inspiratory-flow profile, requires ten separate capsule inhalations per 400-mg dose, has not established pediatric safety or efficacy, and can cause severe bronchospasm even though a supervised tolerance test and bronchodilator pretreatment are used (DAILYMED:2142460a-5afa-f3f3-e063-6394a90ab647). Thus target engagement could increase mucociliary transport without restoring cough force, glottic closure, swallowing, or airway protection; aspiration, infection-driven mucus production, intrinsic lung disease, or respiratory-muscle failure could remain dominant rival explanations, and mobilizing a larger secretion volume could paradoxically increase central-airway burden if extraction fails. No exact-disease prior use or testing was identified, so viability depends on experimentally demonstrating adequate dry-powder deposition, acute airway tolerance, improved airway-surface hydration or sputum rheology, increased radiolabeled mucociliary transport, and—critically—a net reduction rather than redistribution of retained secretions under measured cough-flow conditions.

## mecasermin

Mecasermin is a scientifically testable but highly conditional probe of whether the postnatal head-growth deceleration reported in UNC80 deficiency reflects a modifiable neural-tissue-accrual deficit: it is recombinant human IGF-1, and the prescribing information establishes type 1 IGF-1-receptor activation with mitogenic and organ-growth signaling (https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/021839s033lbl.pdf). The proposed direction is supported at pathway level by a transgenic mouse experiment in which sustained IGF-1 overexpression produced brains 55% larger by postnatal day 55, with increased cell size or apparent cell number and 130% more total myelin (PMID:8386530), by intravenous-tracer evidence that intact IGF-1 can enter brain parenchyma through saturable transport (PMID:11025411), and by a phase 1 Rett study in which subcutaneous mecasermin increased cerebrospinal-fluid IGF-1, although its nonlinear kinetics showed greater peripheral distribution (PMID:24623853). Those observations justify testing whether early IGF-1R stimulation increases neural-progenitor output, lineage-specific cell number, neurite complexity, organoid volume, and myelin markers in an UNC80-deficient model, but they do not show correction of the primary UNC80-NALCN sodium-leak-channel defect or establish an IGF-1-responsive lesion. The disease bridge is especially uncertain because postnatal microcephaly is variably penetrant, frequently accompanies short stature and low BMI, and is inferred mainly from occipitofrontal circumference while MRI can be structurally normal; impaired tissue accrual, altered skull growth, undernutrition or recurrent illness, and tissue loss therefore remain rival explanations (PMCID:PMC4716670; PMCID:PMC6671679; PMCID:PMC5671762). The 55% mouse-brain enlargement is also a supraphysiologic overexpression phenotype rather than pharmacological rescue, the blood-brain-barrier study used intravenous IGF-1 rather than the marketed subcutaneous regimen, and raised cerebrospinal-fluid concentration does not establish regional parenchymal target engagement. Human neurodevelopmental translation is mixed and does not validate brain-growth rescue: a nine-child Phelan-McDermid crossover pilot reported behavioral improvements but no significant change in height, weight, head circumference, or bone age over three months and more adverse events with IGF-1 than placebo (PMID:25685306), whereas a 30-participant Rett crossover trial found no significant treatment-group benefit and deterioration in several clinical and EEG measures (PMID:29560377); both disorders have mechanisms distinct from UNC80 deficiency and neither trial measured brain-tissue accrual. No report or registered trial of mecasermin in exact UNC80 deficiency was identified in targeted searches, so prior use is absent rather than negative evidence. A decisive experiment should apply clinically relevant, developmentally timed exposures to patient-derived neural progenitors or brain organoids with wild-type, vehicle, and IGF-1R-blocked controls, require region- and lineage-resolved tissue gains without edema or a merely systemic/cranium-only growth signal, and test whether electrophysiologic UNC80-NALCN dysfunction remains unchanged; clinical translation would additionally be constrained by twice-daily meal-linked dosing, hypoglycemic seizures, intracranial hypertension, lymphoid hypertrophy, and neoplasia warnings (https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=d5b2b0e1-c523-468d-9a0b-4ae4e4a8dbce).

## doxepin

Doxepin is scientifically viable only as a downstream, phenotype-contingent experimental probe of sleep maintenance, not as a correction of the UNC80–NALCN channel complex: its proposed action is central H1-receptor antagonism, and an H1-receptor knockout experiment found that doxepin increased NREM sleep and longer NREM bouts in wild-type mice but not knockout mice, although the 15 mg/kg mouse exposure is not equivalent to low-dose human treatment (PMID:25498564). Low-dose doxepin improved polysomnographic wake time during sleep, wake after sleep onset, and sleep efficiency in adults with primary insomnia (PMCID:PMC2082089), with maintenance benefit persisting in a 35-day 3- or 6-mg trial (PMCID:PMC3174845); these findings support the ability to suppress wake intrusion but do not show disease-mechanism rescue. The directional bridge is therefore conditional: if human UNC80 deficiency includes excessive histaminergic wake drive during the early sleep period, H1 blockade could lengthen bouts and reduce awakenings, matching the early fragmentation observed in two unc-80 loss-of-function worm alleles (PMCID:PMC13334172). Material contrary evidence is that an earlier worm assay found normal total sleep despite increased sleep-bout response latency (PMCID:PMC6283151), human reports describe heterogeneous difficulty initiating sleep, sleep-wake reversal, and obstructive sleep apnea rather than a demonstrated early-maintenance phenotype (PMCID:PMC4716670; DOI:10.1007/s00439-018-1929-5), and no evidence establishes histamine excess downstream of UNC80 loss. A longer immobile interval could consequently be nonspecific sedation or a motor-readout artefact, especially in severe hypotonia, while circadian dysregulation, respiratory events, seizures, concomitant CNS-active medicines, and caregiving schedules remain rival explanations; doxepin could also worsen the delayed excess-quiescence phase or impair clinically important arousal even if apparent bout consolidation improves. The adult insomnia evidence has limited transfer because neurologically complex patients were not studied, the 35-day trial excluded participants with at least 10 apnea/hypopnea events per hour, the label states that obstructive sleep apnea has not been studied and pediatric safety and effectiveness have not been evaluated (PMCID:PMC3174845; DAILYMED:1bec1223-5239-4eb6-a9e8-62444106d2c0). Exact-disease guidance reports benefit from melatonin and risperidone for some sleep difficulties but not doxepin, and no exact-disease doxepin use or trial was located (NCBI-BOOKSHELF:NBK453434). A discriminating study would first document the human sleep phenotype and then test low-dose exposure for fewer early-night awakenings and longer bouts without disproportionate delayed sleep, altered stimulus arousal, daytime sedation, respiratory compromise, or merely reduced movement; the critical unknowns are human bout-level and circadian physiology, histaminergic involvement, age-appropriate pharmacokinetics and interactions, and whether any benefit survives those safety and measurement controls.

## arimoclomol

Arimoclomol is a plausible allele-specific experimental probe for whether human UNC80 Arg2842Gln remains amenable to chaperone-assisted conformational rescue, but the proposed bridge is indirect: arimoclomol amplifies an already activated HSF1 heat-shock response, and in differentiated human SH-SY5Y cells its combination with the HSF1 activator celastrol enhanced inducible HSPs while constitutive HSPA8 did not change (DOI:10.1007/s12192-016-0708-2); it also increased folding, maturation, activity and localization of mutant glucocerebrosidase in patient cells and a human neuronal model (DOI:10.1016/j.ebiom.2018.11.037), yet that ER/lysosomal client is not evidence that the giant cytosolic scaffold UNC80 is an HSP-responsive client. The disease-side rationale is stronger and proximal: the Arg2910Gln mouse construct equivalent to human Arg2842Gln was expressed comparably to wild type but recovered about 60% less UNC79 by co-immunoprecipitation (DOI:10.1038/s41467-020-17105-8), and structural mapping places this residue in an intramolecular Arg2910-Glu2965 contact (DOI:10.1038/s41467-022-30403-7), making altered local conformation one explanation; a rival explanation is a specific interface defect that increased bulk folding capacity cannot repair. A decisive experiment would use isogenic human neurons with endogenous Arg2842Gln UNC80, verify pharmacodynamic HSP induction, and measure soluble and total UNC80, UNC79 co-immunoprecipitation normalized to both inputs, NALCN-associated UNC79, dendritic localization, surface NALCN and regulated sodium-leak current: increased binary association followed by assembly and current rescue would support the proposed direction, binding-only rescue would expose a post-binding limit, and increased abundance without normalized binding would indicate nonspecific stabilization. Co-immunoprecipitation artefacts from overexpression, altered lysis solubility or global HSP-driven changes in UNC79/NALCN abundance must be excluded; a null could mean that Arg2842Gln is not chaperone-responsive, but could also reflect inadequate baseline HSF1 activation or neuronal exposure and therefore would not by itself distinguish those explanations. Human translation supplies mixed context rather than target validation: a small randomized NPC trial reported slower clinical progression (DOI:10.1002/jimd.12428), while a 239-person phase 3 ALS trial found no functional-survival benefit and more treatment-related discontinuations, with investigators judging that higher dosing would not have been tolerated (DOI:10.1016/S1474-4422(24)00134-0). Oral arimoclomol reaches CSF in a dose-dependent manner, but the FDA label states that its clinical mechanism is unknown and identifies hypersensitivity risks (https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2024/214927s000lbl.pdf), so CSF detection does not establish sufficient intracellular concentration in the relevant developing neurons. No exact-disease use or test was identified, consistent with UNC80 GeneReviews describing management as supportive (NCBI-BOOKSHELF:NBK453434); the critical unknowns are UNC80 chaperone dependence, Arg2842Gln rescueability, the required stress state and concentration, whether molecular rescue propagates through channelosome assembly, and whether intervention after developmental injury can change neuronal function.

## isoflurane

Low- or sub-anesthetic isoflurane is most plausibly an acute mechanistic probe of recruitable residual NALCN current, not a chronic treatment proposal: in mouse CA3 pyramidal neurons, approximately 0.12-0.15 mM isoflurane enhanced NALCN-linked conductance, depolarized resting potential, and increased firing while minimally affecting voltage-gated sodium current, and the associated in vivo hyperactivity was diminished by NALCN knockdown; in chemosensitive retrotrapezoid neurons, approximately 0.20-0.50 mM isoflurane increased sodium-leak conductance and firing, with the effect strongly reduced by NALCN silencing or gadolinium (DOI:10.1016/j.isci.2019.100760; DOI:10.1097/ALN.0000000000003493). This direction is relevant because UNC80-null mouse hippocampal neurons retained a small basal NALCN-dependent current despite a fall from 12.0 +/- 1.5 pA in wild type to 2.4 +/- 0.4 pA in knockout, while a disease-analogous C-terminal truncation retained robust whole-cell current despite loss of UNC79 recruitment and dendritic localization (DOI:10.1038/s41467-020-17105-8); the inferential bridge is therefore that an allele and neuronal subtype retaining correctly localized, responsive pores might convert pharmacological current recruitment into useful depolarization. The principal null is mechanistically important: 0.4-0.5 mM isoflurane did not increase current or conductance in NALCN-only transfected HEK cells, so the neuronal effect is not established as direct pore activation and may require UNC79-UNC80, another cellular component, or an indirect pathway that UNC80 deficiency itself disrupts (DOI:10.1016/j.isci.2019.100760). Regional and off-target effects further narrow the interpretation: 0.1 mM increased hippocampal network excitability while reducing neocortical excitability in rat slices (DOI:10.1371/journal.pone.0039346), and clinically relevant concentrations inhibit transient and persistent neuronal sodium currents (DOI:10.1097/ALN.0000000000002753), consistent with the loss of excitation and suppression of firing at higher isoflurane exposure. NALCN knockdown and sodium-substitution controls support NALCN participation, but gadolinium is nonspecific and neither the mouse slice studies nor network optical signals exclude disinhibition or other anesthetic targets as contributors. No controlled test in UNC80-deficient human neurons or animals, and no disease-specific therapeutic use of isoflurane, was located; the decisive experiment is consequently a concentration-resolved comparison in isogenic UNC80-mutant and corrected disease-relevant neurons, with NALCN knockdown and matched measurements of NALCN current, resting potential, firing, and subcellular localization, rejecting the route if enhancement disappears with UNC80 loss or occurs only at exposures that inhibit Nav currents or impair network output.

## sodium phenylbutyrate

Sodium phenylbutyrate is a clinically used nitrogen-scavenging prodrug with broad proteostasis and transcriptional actions, and it is scientifically reasonable as an experimental probe—not an established treatment—of two separable UNC80-deficiency lesions: whether the human Arg2842Gln-equivalent UNC80 conformer can recover UNC79 association, and whether residual synthesis-competent NALCN complexes can regain neurite or axonal localization. The disease-side bridge is specific but still inferential: Arg2910Gln, equivalent to human Arg2842Gln, was present at a level comparable to wild type yet recovered about 60% less UNC79 by co-immunoprecipitation, while the wild-type structure places this residue in an intramolecular UNC80 contact rather than proving a direct UNC79 contact; independently, unc-80 loss markedly reduced axonal NCA-1 despite retained transcript, and UNC79–UNC80 promotes mammalian NALCN surface localization (DOI:10.1038/s41467-020-17105-8; DOI:10.1038/s41467-022-30403-7; DOI:10.1371/journal.pbio.0060055). The drug-side evidence is transferable only at the process level: 4-phenylbutyrate restored ER export, surface expression, uptake, and neurite delivery for several folding-deficient creatine-transporter variants, increased insertion or membrane trafficking of ENaC and wild-type GABAA receptor subunits, and can alter trafficking by downregulating Hsc70 rather than by directly stabilizing the assayed client protein (DOI:10.1016/j.neuropharm.2019.03.015; DOI:10.1074/jbc.m702384200; DOI:10.1111/epi.17779; DOI:10.1152/ajpcell.2000.278.2.C259). Thus a selective increase in the mutant-to-wild-type UNC79 co-association ratio, followed by increased NALCN-associated UNC79, or a compartment-resolved increase in axonal NALCN with preserved channel-complex composition, would support the proposed direction; increased total UNC80, generic stress-marker changes, bulk somatic current, or altered excitability without spatial rescue would not. The premise is allele-limited because absent or severely truncated UNC80 cannot be chaperoned into existence, and it is contradicted by mutation-dependent nonresponse and even increased aggregation of another mutant client after phenylbutyrate, requiring detergent-solubility, aggregate, cell-viability, total-protein, and wild-type controls (DOI:10.1165/rcmb.2012-0003oc). Co-immunoprecipitation changes could also reflect altered abundance, detergent solubility, epitope accessibility, or global chaperone expression rather than corrected binding, while HDAC-dependent transcription, Hsc70 modulation, nitrogen scavenging, and stress responses are rival explanations for any phenotypic gain. Rat experiments support blood-to-brain transport through MCT1, but they do not establish human neuronal exposure; the approved oral label reports a peak within about one hour and a phenylbutyrate half-life of about 0.77 hours, leaving uncertain whether relevant brain concentrations and exposure duration can be achieved, and it also records developmental neurotoxicity of high-dose phenylacetate in rat pups (DOI:10.1007/s11095-016-1912-6; DAILYMED:463a36fa-3eb2-4326-8bd0-c8c7a11bca3a). No exact-disease use or testing of phenylbutyrate was identified, and the current UNC80 GeneReview describes supportive management rather than a disease-modifying pharmacotherapy (NCBI-BOOKSHELF:NBK453434); decisive next evidence therefore requires isogenic human neurons with genotype-matched null and missense controls, blinded compartment-resolved localization and complex-assembly assays, exposure measurements, washout, and electrophysiology explicitly secondary to molecular rescue.

## amifampridine

Amifampridine is a scientifically testable but sharply conditional symptomatic strategy for UNC80-deficiency hypotonia: broad presynaptic voltage-gated potassium-current blockade can prolong motor-terminal depolarization and increase calcium-coupled acetylcholine release and endplate-potential amplitude (PMID:1964819), and randomized withdrawal data in Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome show rapid human muscle benefit when the primary lesion is deficient presynaptic release (PMID:26852139). The disease-specific directional bridge is model-based rather than human: unc-80/NCA loss in C. elegans can preserve somatic calcium activity while reducing synaptic-region calcium transients and neuromuscular transmission (PMCID:PMC2265767), so amifampridine could amplify a residual terminal-release reserve and raise quantal content, evoked compound-muscle response, and force without restoring neuronal resting potential, UNC79 recruitment, NALCN conductance, or developmental wiring. That distinction is decisive because human UNC80 disease establishes congenital hypotonia and variant-dependent NALCN dysfunction but does not establish a neuromuscular-junction defect; preserved reflexes, mixed axial hypotonia with appendicular spasticity, nonspecific muscle findings, and profound central developmental impairment leave central circuit failure, weakness, deconditioning, and secondary muscle change as strong rivals (PMCID:PMC4716670; PMCID:PMC4716667; PMCID:PMC7335163). Translation outside classic presynaptic disease is modest: a short, open-label-responder-enriched crossover study randomized only 12 ambulatory SMA type 3 participants, found a statistically significant 0.792-point HFMSE advantage that was below the cited clinically important range, and found no 6-minute-walk benefit, so practice, selection, and acute nonspecific excitation remain plausible explanations (PMID:35763114). Exact-disease prior work also sets a useful negative context: an acute 743-drug unc-80 worm screen confirmed other behavioral hits, chiefly liranaftate and atorvastatin among the lower-side-effect hits, and explicitly cautioned that its phenotypes and hits might reflect secondary systems; amifampridine was not reported as a hit, although the article does not establish that it was present in the library, so this is not an interpretable compound-specific null (PMID:39773880). Exposure favors the proposed peripheral-compensation interpretation because 3,4-diaminopyridine penetrated rat cerebrospinal fluid significantly less than 4-aminopyridine (PMID:6744035), while oral exposure is brief and highly variable: the terminal half-life is 1.8-2.5 hours, NAT2 poor metabolizers have 5.6-9-fold higher AUC, and renal impairment can raise AUC two- to threefold; importantly for a disorder in which seizures occur, the label contraindicates a seizure history and reports seizures at recommended doses in about 2% of recipients (https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2024/208078s012lbl.pdf). The critical preclinical test is an isogenic patient-derived motor-neuron-muscle co-culture with simultaneous terminal action-potential duration, presynaptic calcium, quantal content, postsynaptic acetylcholine responsiveness, neuronal resting potential, and evoked force: increased release and force with unchanged resting potential would support compartment-limited compensation, whereas failure despite intact postsynaptic responsiveness would argue that no recruitable terminal reserve exists; any subsequent clinical experiment would need objective repetitive-stimulation or single-fiber physiology, separate axial and appendicular outcomes, seizure-risk controls, and NAT2/renal exposure management, and should not be interpreted as disease modification. No human use or trial of amifampridine in UNC80 deficiency was identified.

## pyridostigmine

Pyridostigmine is a peripherally acting reversible acetylcholinesterase inhibitor that prolongs acetylcholine action at recruited neuromuscular junctions, so the proposed direction is a conditional compensatory one: if UNC80-deficiency oral-motor failure includes inadequate force, fatigability, or transmission reserve downstream of otherwise usable motor commands, transiently increasing end-plate drive could improve chewing, bolus transfer, pharyngeal contraction, or swallow endurance without correcting UNC80/NALCN channel-complex biology [DAILYMED:0b296555-c32f-42db-b5f3-ea981a3dd2f8]. Disease reports establish poor sucking, dysphagia, hypotonia, and tube dependence but provide neither instrumental phase localization nor evidence of neuromuscular-junction dysfunction [PMCID:PMC6671679; DOI:10.1002/ajmg.a.64224]. Translation is biologically possible but sharply context-dependent: in anti-AChR-positive myasthenia gravis, where transmission failure is established, a 19-adult randomized crossover trial found better MG impairment with pyridostigmine than placebo, whereas a 35-patient spinal-muscular-atrophy crossover trial found no significant benefit on either primary motor endpoint despite better self-reported fatigability and endurance and more adverse events [DOI:10.1212/WNL.0000000000214865; DOI:10.1093/braincomms/fcac324]. Swallow-specific evidence is weaker: a five-patient acute manometry crossover showed greater distal esophageal contractile vigor and more peristaltic swallows but no other pharyngeal or esophageal manometric change, and a single Guillain–Barré case showed less pharyngeal retention and aspiration 30 minutes after 60 mg, although late spontaneous neurological recovery and repeated testing remain rival explanations [DOI:10.1007/s00455-020-10243-7; PMCID:PMC3309320]. The supplied 40-adult post-stroke placebo trial reported improvement after 60 mg three times daily, yet both groups changed on its swallowing scale, the reported ASHA/FCM direction is internally inconsistent with the stated scale in which higher levels mean better oral feeding, baseline prior-stroke history was imbalanced, no instrumental swallowing endpoint was used, and aspiration-pneumonia incidence did not differ; recovery, rehabilitation, and esophageal rather than oral-motor effects therefore remain plausible artefacts or alternatives [PMCID:PMC9860206]. Exposure fits a peripheral test rather than a central rescue: the label reports poor oral bioavailability, an approximately three-hour half-life, negligible brain entry, renal elimination, and muscarinic hypersalivation and increased bronchial secretion, so variable meal-time exposure, renal function, bradycardia or gastrointestinal intolerance, and secretion-related airway burden are material constraints in a population already vulnerable to aspiration [DAILYMED:0b296555-c32f-42db-b5f3-ea981a3dd2f8]. Central pattern-generation or sensory-integration failure, dyskinesia, posture, reflux, and pharyngeal-airway discoordination could all dominate despite adequate peripheral transmission. No UNC80-specific pyridostigmine use or testing was identified, and current disease-specific management describes feeding therapy or gastrostomy rather than pharmacological augmentation [NCBI-BOOKSHELF:NBK453434]. A scientifically informative test would therefore require baseline and on-dose VFSS or FEES with phase-resolved residue and penetration-aspiration measures, oral-force or task EMG/fatigability readouts, secretion and respiratory monitoring, and pharmacokinetic/renal-aware dosing; the critical unknowns are whether a peripheral transmission deficit exists, whether safe target engagement can be achieved in affected children, and whether any force or endurance gain improves airway-safe intake rather than merely motility or secretions.

## Glycopyrrolate

Glycopyrrolate is a peripherally acting quaternary antimuscarinic that can reduce the volume of oral saliva without repairing swallowing coordination, so its directional bridge to UNC80 deficiency is conditional: recurrent aspiration is documented in the disorder, and if saliva is a substantial aspirate component, reducing salivary volume could reduce salivary airway entry while leaving food-, liquid-, or refluxate aspiration unchanged (DOI:10.1016/j.ajhg.2015.11.013; DOI:10.1016/j.pmr.2008.06.001). The pharmacodynamic premise is credible—an eight-week randomized pediatric neurodisability trial found a 73.7% modified Teacher's Drooling Scale response versus 17.6% with placebo (PMID:22298950), and an earlier double-blind crossover study showed a mean score of 1.85 versus 6.33 with placebo—but these caregiver/observer ratings quantify external drooling rather than swallowed secretion, salivary airway entry, aspiration frequency, pneumonia, or lung injury, and the earlier study explicitly notes that muscarinic blockade does not address impaired swallowing (DOI:10.1001/archpedi.154.12.1214). Translation is further limited by exposure and selection: a single-dose study in six healthy children found median oral bioavailability of only 3.3% with a 1.3–13.3% range (PMID:9783332), while the 24-week open-label study excluded important high-risk comorbidities, achieved a drooling response in 52.3%, recorded treatment-emergent adverse events in 89% and discontinuation for adverse events in 13.9%, and observed one aspiration-pneumonia death within 30 days after treatment that investigators judged unrelated, providing neither a protective respiratory signal nor causal evidence of harm (PMID:22298951). Excessive dryness or thickened secretions caused discontinuation in the crossover study, a material concern in UNC80 deficiency because hypotonia-associated ineffective cough and secretion retention are plausible rival or interacting respiratory mechanisms (DOI:10.1001/archpedi.154.12.1214; DOI:10.7759/cureus.4395); constipation, vomiting, urinary retention, and gastrointestinal motility effects also matter where constipation or delayed gastric emptying may coexist. No exact-disease use, instrumental salivary-aspiration test, or UNC80-specific glycopyrrolate trial was located, so viability rests on a falsifiable compensatory experiment: establish salivary aspiration at baseline, demonstrate reduced salivary airway entry rather than merely drier clothing during individualized exposure, and concurrently measure secretion viscosity, cough clearance, respiratory events, bowel and bladder effects; a food- or refluxate-dominant pattern, unchanged instrumental salivary aspiration, or worsened retained secretions would refute the proposed bridge.

## dalfampridine

Dalfampridine (4-aminopyridine) is a broad potassium-channel blocker that can prolong axonal depolarization and enhance transmitter release, so reducing an opposing outward current could, in principle, compensate for deficient UNC80-dependent inward leak and increase soma-to-synapse propagation or motor output without repairing UNC80 or NALCN (DAILYMED:ed826777-b8c4-4d35-aa64-a4c28452baa8; DOI:10.1113/jphysiol.1982.sp014180). The disease bridge is directionally plausible but indirect: a patient variant and UNC80-null neurons show markedly reduced NALCN current, whereas C. elegans loss of the complex preserves somatic activity in some neurons while reducing presynaptic calcium events and neuromuscular transmission (DOI:10.1016/j.ajhg.2015.11.004; DOI:10.1038/s41467-020-17105-8; DOI:10.1371/journal.pbio.0060055). Positive empirical translation is limited to other pathologies: in a randomized multiple-sclerosis trial, sustained-release fampridine produced a timed-walk response in 35% versus 8% on placebo, demonstrating that systemic exposure can improve motor performance in a subset with demyelinating conduction failure but not that it corrects an UNC80 channelosome defect (DOI:10.1016/S0140-6736(09)60442-6). Material counterevidence narrows the hypothesis: 4-aminopyridine caused spontaneous or multiple firing in some demyelinated axons, and higher concentrations suppressed conduction, while the label warns of seizures (DOI:10.1016/0006-8993(86)90652-9; DOI:10.1016/s0306-4522(96)00477-0; DAILYMED:ed826777-b8c4-4d35-aa64-a4c28452baa8). Exposure and target interpretation are especially uncertain because cloned human Kv1.1, Kv1.2, and Kv1.4 channels in HEK293 cells had IC50 values of 242-399 µM, leaving unresolved which native neuronal potassium currents are engaged at a tolerable CNS exposure and whether such engagement is spatially selective enough to help deficient cells (DOI:10.3109/21556660.2013.791623). Whole-cell current reduction is also not universal across UNC80 alleles: a disease-associated truncation retained whole-cell NALCN current but lost dendritic localization and UNC79 recruitment, so assembly, trafficking, developmental wiring, or cell-type-specific conductance balance could be rate-limiting rivals that potassium blockade cannot repair (DOI:10.1038/s41467-020-17105-8). Moreover, enhanced release at both excitatory and inhibitory synapses, repetitive firing, or isolated terminal release without restored soma-to-synapse coupling could mimic benefit or worsen circuit organization; the unresolved central-versus-peripheral basis of hypotonia further weakens direct transfer to muscle tone. No exact-disease dalfampridine or 4-aminopyridine use or testing was found in the packet corpus or a targeted PubMed title/abstract search. Viability therefore rests on a genotype-matched, patient-derived dose-response experiment measuring resting potential, paired somatic and presynaptic activity, transmitter output, and hyperexcitability in deficient and corrected neurons; selective restoration of temporally coupled propagation within a seizure-free exposure window would support compensation, whereas target engagement without rescue, tonic firing, depolarization block, or equal effects in corrected controls would favor the rival explanations.

## teprenone

Teprenone (geranylgeranylacetone, GGA) is a scientifically usable but highly provisional pharmacological probe of whether increasing cellular heat-shock capacity can improve the Arg2842Gln UNC80 conformational ensemble: this allele-equivalent retains UNC80 expression yet recovers about 60% less UNC79 by co-immunoprecipitation, while UNC80 normally recruits UNC79 into the NALCN complex, so an input-normalized increase in variant UNC80-UNC79 association followed by increased NALCN-associated UNC79 would be directionally consistent with rescue of the focal binding defect and its propagation to channelosome assembly (DOI:10.1038/s41467-020-17105-8; DOI:10.1038/s41467-022-30403-7). GGA directly binds the HSP70 C-terminal substrate-binding region, suppresses HSP70 chaperone activity in vitro, and releases HSF1, providing a mechanistic route to heat-shock-gene induction but also warning that the compound is not simply an HSP70 functional enhancer (DOI:10.1016/j.bbrc.2006.12.031); oral GGA increased rat-brain HSP70 (DOI:10.1016/j.neulet.2005.11.065), and translation beyond gastric tissue is supported by a small placebo-controlled cardiac-surgery study in which 400 mg/day for three days increased atrial HSPA1 and HSPB1, although total and phosphorylated HSF1, HSPD1, and HSPA5 were unchanged (DOI:10.1016/j.hrthm.2019.07.010), while 150 mg/day also increased human gastric-mucosal HSP70 (DOI:10.1159/000106756). These observations do not establish neuronal target engagement at clinically relevant exposure: brain induction used a much larger rodent dose, human brain concentrations are unreported, and in four patients food increased systemic AUC after 100 mg by 30- to 45-fold, indicating unusually strong administration-dependent exposure (PMID:6885198). The apparent positive endpoint could instead reflect increased UNC80 or UNC79 abundance, altered solubility or epitope accessibility, generalized stress, or co-immunoprecipitation artefact rather than restored affinity; moreover, at >=100 micromolar GGA induced GRP78, unfolded-protein-response signaling, and mesangial-cell apoptosis without increasing HSP70, establishing concentration- and cell-context-dependent HSP70-independent toxicity (DOI:10.1124/mol.107.039164). A decisive experiment therefore needs concentration and time courses in an Arg2842Gln human-neuronal model with total and soluble UNC80/UNC79, cytotoxicity, HSPA1 induction, input-normalized co-immunoprecipitation plus an orthogonal interaction assay, NALCN-associated UNC79, surface localization, and regulated current, alongside HSF1/HSPA1 perturbation to separate heat-shock dependence from direct HSP70 inhibition or other GGA actions; no prior teprenone use or testing in exact UNC80 deficiency was identified, and it remains unknown whether this variant is chaperone-responsive or whether improved binary association is sufficient for assembly and function.

## levosimendan

Levosimendan is a plausible distal, symptom-focused probe rather than a correction of UNC80–NALCN pathology: in skinned human diaphragm fibres it increased submaximal force by about 25% by increasing calcium sensitivity without increasing maximal force (PMID:18990676), and acute randomised testing in healthy adults improved diaphragm neuromechanical efficiency by 21% during inspiratory loading (PMID:21960535). An UNC80-deficient child was reported to require cough assistance because hypotonia prevented secretion clearance (DOI:10.7759/cureus.4395), while paediatric neuromuscular physiology supports reduced inspiratory volume and expiratory flow as contributors to ineffective cough (PMCID:PMC10928549); therefore, if residual neural and bulbar activation reaches respiratory myofibres, calcium sensitisation could increase pre-cough inflation or expiratory pressure. That directional bridge remains inferential because UNC80 loss primarily impairs neuronal channelosome function and motor output (PMCID:PMC4716670), and the limiting lesion could instead be central respiratory drive, soma-to-synapse propagation, glottic closure, bulbar discoordination or aspiration, airway obstruction or secretion load, or fixed deconditioning, so stronger diaphragm pressure need not improve expulsive flow. Translation is mixed and substantially negative: a small phase II ALS crossover trial missed its primary sitting slow-vital-capacity endpoint and produced only a post-hoc, period-adjusted supine signal (DOI:10.1136/jnnp-2018-320288); a randomised study in 39 mechanically ventilated adults found no improvement in diaphragm contractile efficiency (PMCID:PMC6773912); and the 496-participant phase III REFALS trial found no benefit in supine slow vital capacity at 12 weeks (difference 0.26 percentage points, 95% CI -2.03 to 2.55, p=0.83) or function and survival at 48 weeks, while increased heart rate occurred in 33% versus 7% and headache in 29% versus 22% (DOI:10.1016/S1474-4422(21)00242-8). The acute healthy-volunteer effect may therefore depend on preserved activation, task, route, exposure, or population rather than represent robust efficacy in chronic neuromuscular weakness; lack of blood–brain-barrier penetration is consistent with the intended peripheral action but precludes rescue of core neural drive, and no exact-disease levosimendan use or test was identified. A discriminating UNC80-deficiency experiment would pair transdiaphragmatic or maximal inspiratory and expiratory pressures with cough-flow waveform and peak cough flow at a haemodynamically tolerated exposure: increased pressure without improved cough flow would favour glottic or airway rivals, whereas no pressure change would argue against adequate muscle sensitisation or exposure in this setting.

## Cilostazol

Cilostazol is an orally available PDE3 inhibitor whose established pharmacology raises cAMP while producing reversible antiplatelet and vasodilator effects [DAILYMED:77e77eaf-5556-43ac-844e-c0e29f02fe07]. In adults with prior stroke, a small randomized double-blind crossover comparison with aspirin reported increased airway and serum substance P and shorter latency of the evoked swallowing reflex [PMID:18554369]; a chronic cerebral-hypoperfusion rat study independently shortened elicited-swallow latency, increased swallow number, reduced lung bacterial colonies, and linked the effect to CREB phosphorylation, tyrosine-hydroxylase expression, and preservation of striatal substance P, although that model does not establish the same pathway in UNC80 deficiency [PMID:19032975]. Because recurrent aspiration is directly reported in biallelic UNC80 disease while the failing swallow phase remains unknown [DOI:10.1016/j.ajhg.2015.11.013], the directional bridge is testable but conditional: if delayed pharyngeal sensory triggering contributes materially, shortening trigger latency could narrow the interval for airway invasion, whereas oral-motor incoordination, inadequate laryngeal closure, post-swallow residue, reflux, impaired cough, secretion retention, or silent aspiration could dominate and would not necessarily improve. Translation beyond the latency assay is mixed and vulnerable to artefact: a 189-patient retrospective acute-stroke study observed pneumonia in 3/48 cilostazol recipients versus 24/141 nonrecipients [PMID:22877690], but treatment was not randomized and pneumonia is influenced by stroke severity, feeding route, recurrent stroke, and supportive care; conversely, a contemporary nationwide propensity-matched analysis of 65,125 pairs found more 90-day aspiration-pneumonia readmissions with cilostazol than with aspirin or clopidogrel (1.5% versus 1.2%), providing a material clinical null/negative result despite residual claims-data confounding and absence of instrumental swallow endpoints [PMID:37586338]. No cilostazol use, swallowing study, or aspiration-prevention trial in UNC80 deficiency was located, and neither substance-P deficiency nor delayed swallow triggering has been demonstrated in this disorder. Exposure is especially uncertain because the human swallowing and pneumonia evidence is in adults with acquired vascular disease, whereas many affected individuals are children; pediatric safety and effectiveness are unestablished, and the antiplatelet, tachycardic, hypotensive, interaction, and bleeding liabilities, together with the heart-failure contraindication, make direct physiological confirmation by swallow-provocation latency followed by videofluoroscopy essential before interpreting any event-level benefit [DAILYMED:77e77eaf-5556-43ac-844e-c0e29f02fe07].

## salbutamol

Salbutamol is a beta-2-adrenoceptor agonist that would not repair the primary UNC80-NALCN channelosome defect but could provide a downstream compensation if reduced skeletal-muscle reserve materially amplifies generalized hypotonia: UNC80 case series establish persistent hypotonia and some weakness or wasting, while the available molecular and animal evidence points mainly to impaired neuronal excitability, sustained motor-circuit output, and synaptic transmission rather than a demonstrated primary myopathy (PMCID:PMC4716670; PMCID:PMC4716667; PMCID:PMC2265767). The directional bridge is therefore conditional—beta-2-adrenergic stimulation would need to increase force produced from residual neural drive, not merely muscle size. A small controlled study in healthy young men reported muscle-group-specific increases in quadriceps strength and inspiratory pressure, with no grip-strength or lean-mass change, supporting biological responsiveness but also showing heterogeneity (PMID:1335400). Material nulls weaken translation: in randomized elite athletes, 8 mg oral salbutamol improved short sprint peak power yet changed neither maximal voluntary contraction nor isometric endurance (PMID:25077918), and 11 weeks at 16 mg/day augmented type-IIa-fiber hypertrophy without additional muscle-force gain (PMID:33357007). These discrepancies leave learning, voluntary activation, glycolytic or sprint effects, training interaction, and measurement variability as rivals to a true increase in intrinsic contractile force; hypertrophy is specifically an unreliable surrogate. A one-year placebo-controlled study in adults with spinal muscular atrophy used 4 mg three times daily and reported molecular and exploratory motor signals (PMID:30593463), but its increase in full-length SMN2 provides a disease-specific rival mechanism absent from UNC80 deficiency, so it does not establish a general muscle-reserve effect. At a supratherapeutic 16 mg/day during resistance training, salbutamol increased lean-mass gain but impaired muscle oxidative markers and cardiopulmonary-fitness improvement and produced echocardiographic wall-thickness signals (PMID:42274909), indicating that an exposure sufficient for anabolism may trade against endurance or cardiovascular tolerability. No report or trial of salbutamol/albuterol in UNC80 deficiency was identified. Viability therefore turns on a blinded exposure-response experiment that separates motor-unit-normalized and electrically evoked limb and respiratory force from muscle cross-sectional area, spontaneous neural drive, fatigue, and training; hypertrophy without force, a response confined to supratherapeutic exposure, or no benefit in UNC80-relevant neuromuscular models would reject the proposed compensation, while the safe force-active exposure and the fraction of UNC80 disability attributable to muscle rather than central drive remain critical unknowns.

## epothilone D

Epothilone D is scientifically useful here principally as a discriminating transport-capacity probe, not as an established rescue: it binds luminal beta-tubulin and promotes polymerization (DOI:10.1016/j.brainresbull.2022.10.008), crosses the mouse blood-brain barrier, persists longer in brain than blood, and at 1 mg/kg increased brain acetylated tubulin seven days after dosing (DOI:10.1016/j.phrs.2010.12.002); in aged PS19 tau-transgenic mice, low-dose treatment increased axonal microtubule density and fast axonal transport while reducing axonal dystrophy (DOI:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4922-11.2012). The proposed UNC80 bridge is directionally coherent only as an inference: L2654*-truncated UNC80 is retained in the soma and absent from axons and dendrites despite robust whole-cell NALCN current, so increasing usable microtubule polymer might raise its neurite-to-soma ratio without changing bulk current (DOI:10.1038/s41467-020-17105-8). That bridge is nevertheless weak because no microtubule defect has been shown in UNC80 deficiency, the localization result comes from overexpressed tagged constructs in cultured mouse hippocampal neurons rather than endogenous patient neurons, and the truncation deletes the C-terminal localization region and UNC79-interaction interval; an irreplaceable cargo-recognition or complex-assembly defect is therefore a stronger rival explanation than insufficient track capacity. Moreover, epothilone D does not simply create longer, straighter tracks: super-resolution analysis in PC12 axon-like processes found increased microtubule density but decreased individual length and straightness (DOI:10.1016/j.brainresbull.2022.10.008), so target engagement alone cannot be assumed to improve UNC80 transport. Target engagement without UNC80 redistribution is the decisive mechanistic null, while redistribution without restored local dendritic NALCN physiology would leave disease relevance unresolved; any general axonal or behavioral benefit could instead reflect nonspecific cytoskeletal remodeling. Exposure is a major constraint because epothilone D reduced viability, growth, and complexity in immature cortical neurons and altered mitochondrial transport at sublethal exposures in mature cultures (DOI:10.1038/s41598-020-57718-z), while an adult oncology phase I study found dose-related reversible neurotoxicity and dose-limiting sensory neuropathy at much higher anticancer exposures that do not define a developmental-neurology window (DOI:10.1007/s10637-011-9765-7). A completed 40-participant mild-Alzheimer trial tested nine weekly intravenous doses of BMS-241027 at 0.003-0.03 mg/kg but posted no results (NCT01492374), and no exact-disease use or testing in UNC80 deficiency was identified. A credible experiment therefore requires disease-relevant neurons, pharmacodynamic confirmation, quantitative endogenous or equivalently validated truncated-UNC80 neurite-to-soma localization, local dendritic channelosome readouts, and a dose range that jointly excludes mitochondrial-transport and neurite-growth toxicity.

## progesterone

Progesterone is a plausible mechanistic probe, rather than an established treatment, for testing whether increasing retained NALCN pore abundance can enlarge residual conductance in UNC80 deficiency. In the key human myometrial study, 100 nM progesterone acting through PRA or PRB increased NALCN mRNA in receptor-expressing cells and estrogen-plus-progesterone restored a Gd3+/NMDG-sensitive, NALCN-dominated current relative to estrogen alone; however, progesterone alone did not increase NALCN mRNA in the HM6ERMS2 model lacking induced receptor, estrogen-plus-progesterone produced NALCN mRNA similar to vehicle, and NALCN protein was not significantly above vehicle, making the signal partly reversal of estrogen repression rather than demonstrated suprabaseline surface-channel amplification (DOI:10.1152/ajpendo.00320.2019). A neuronal genomic bridge is biologically possible because 100 nM progesterone drove nuclear progesterone-receptor translocation and RU-486-sensitive gene induction in cultured dorsal-root-ganglion neurons (DOI:10.1091/mbc.11.7.2283), but this does not establish NALCN as a progesterone-receptor target in relevant CNS neurons. The disease-side premise is stronger but incomplete: UNC80-null mouse hippocampal neurons retain a small NALCN-dependent current and extra NALCN expression increased it, whereas a disease-analogous truncation preserved whole-cell current while losing UNC79 recruitment and dendritic localization, so additional pore could help some alleles yet fail when assembly, gating, or neurite delivery is limiting (DOI:10.1038/s41467-020-17105-8). Exposure is conceivable because radiolabeled progesterone entered mouse brain after intravenous or intranasal delivery, but rapid degradation and route- and region-dependent kinetics leave human neuronal target engagement unknown (DOI:10.1016/j.ejphar.2010.05.033). Functional readouts would also be confounded at high exposure: 50 µM progesterone rapidly and reversibly inhibited voltage-gated sodium current by 47% and also inhibited potassium and GABA(A) currents in rat striatal neurons (DOI:10.1016/j.mcn.2011.07.002), while endocrine actions and neuroactive metabolites provide further rival explanations. No exact-disease use or testing was identified. The decisive experiment is an allele-stratified panel of patient-derived CNS neurons exposed at clinically attainable free concentrations, with progesterone-receptor blockade or knockdown and measurements of NALCN RNA, total and surface protein, subcellular localization, NALCN-specific current, resting potential, and synaptic output; failure of receptor-dependent surface-current gain despite RNA induction would identify assembly or trafficking as the bottleneck, whereas any electrical improvement must be separated from acute off-target channel effects.

## tauroursodeoxycholic acid

Tauroursodeoxycholic acid (TUDCA) is a defensible chemical-chaperone probe, rather than an established therapy, for the specific possibility that UNC80 Arg2842Gln creates a recoverable C-terminal conformer: the mouse-equivalent Arg2910Gln construct retained total expression but recovered about 60% less UNC79 by co-immunoprecipitation, while the intact-complex structure places Arg2910 in an intramolecular UNC80 Arg2910–Glu2965 contact, making an indirect packing defect plausible but not demonstrating misfolding, aggregation, or a direct UNC79-interface lesion (DOI:10.1038/s41467-020-17105-8; DOI:10.1038/s41467-022-30403-7). TUDCA reduced stress-induced aggregation and hydrophobic-probe binding in generic proteins, and a follow-up study again found anti-aggregation activity, but these experiments used stressed BSA and HepG2 cells and concentrations extending to 5–10 mM, so they do not establish native-state rescue of the very large cytosolic UNC80 scaffold and also leave PERK activation, anti-apoptotic signalling, altered degradation, or nonspecific bile-salt effects as rival explanations (DOI:10.1016/j.abb.2014.12.031; DOI:10.1038/s41598-017-03940-1). The decisive experiment is therefore a matched concentration-response study of wild-type and Arg2910Gln/Arg2842Gln UNC80, requiring an increased mutant-to-wild-type UNC79 co-immunoprecipitation ratio with unchanged soluble input UNC80, no increase in insoluble UNC80, and no change in UNC79 abundance, followed only then by NALCN-associated UNC79 and channel-complex readouts; parallel vehicle, wild-type, unrelated missense, lysis/pull-down, viability, and aggregation controls are needed because altered expression, survival, solubility, or assay recovery could mimic stronger binding. Systemic translation is uncertain: a rat stroke study detected brain bile acid after 400 mg/kg intravenous TUDCA but only in an ischemia-reperfusion setting and at low measured brain abundance, whereas oral 2 g/day was tolerable for 54 weeks in a small ALS trial without showing that neuronal free TUDCA reaches the millimolar concentrations used in aggregation assays (DOI:10.1097/00004647-200204000-00010; DOI:10.1111/ene.12664). No direct TUDCA experiment or prior use in UNC80 deficiency, no Arg2842Gln patient-neuron study, and no evidence that improving this binary association restores channel localization or current were found; a null binding result under exposure-confirmed, non-toxic conditions would favor a specific interface/packing defect that broad anti-aggregation action cannot rescue.

## ricolinostat

Ricolinostat (ACY-1215) directly inhibits HDAC6, with crystallography showing active-site binding and a selectivity contribution from its bulky capping group (DOI:10.1073/pnas.1718823114); oral dosing in people produced systemic exposure and increased acetylated tubulin in peripheral lymphocytes, although higher exposure also began to affect the class-I-HDAC marker acetylated histone (DOI:10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-16-2526). In stressed primary cortical neurons, ACY-1215 prevented tubulin deacetylation and HDAC6 inhibitors restored BDNF transport velocity, supporting the proposed direction of increasing microtubule-dependent cargo transport (DOI:10.1038/s41419-019-1920-7). The disease-side observation is nevertheless different: UNC80 L2654* is retained in the soma because truncation removes a mapped C-terminal localization function, while the same protein retains NALCN association and robust whole-cell leak current but loses UNC79 recruitment (DOI:10.1038/s41467-020-17105-8). Thus, increasing microtubule motor engagement could raise the neurite-to-soma ratio only if truncated UNC80 still loads onto a competent motor-adaptor pathway; neither that loading nor disease-associated tubulin hypoacetylation or HDAC6 overactivity has been demonstrated, and greater track capacity cannot necessarily replace deleted cargo-localization information. The gp120 experiment is an injury model rather than an UNC80-cargo experiment, so its transport rescue could reflect reversal of stress-induced cytoskeletal damage, and HDAC6 inhibition also changes non-tubulin substrates. Exposure is a material negative: ricolinostat poorly penetrated mouse brain and failed to reverse a cisplatin-induced cognitive deficit at a schedule that was active in peripheral neuropathy, whereas a brain-penetrant HDAC6 inhibitor was effective (DOI:10.1186/s40478-018-0604-3). No exact-disease use or test was identified. A discriminating experiment would establish acetylated-tubulin target engagement in UNC80-mutant patient-derived neurons, quantify endogenous UNC80 and NALCN-complex localization in neurites versus soma, and then measure dendritic NALCN-dependent physiology; unchanged localization despite target engagement would falsify the transport-capacity bridge, whereas redistribution without physiological rescue would favor the rival UNC79-assembly or local-channel-regulation lesion.

## carbachol

Carbachol is scientifically plausible only as a discriminating probe of residual channel function, not as a repair of the UNC80-UNC79 scaffold: 100 micromolar carbachol evoked an M3-receptor-associated inward current in NALCN/M3R reconstitution, and a later HEK293T study found the response to be sodium-carrying and reduced by NALCN knockdown (DOI:10.1038/embor.2009.125; DOI:10.1111/bph.15104). The desired direction is relevant because UNC80-null mouse hippocampal neurons retained a small NALCN-dependent current, while a disease-analogous C-terminal truncation preserved whole-cell current despite losing UNC79 recruitment and neuritic localization, so some alleles could retain pores whose somatic current can be amplified (DOI:10.1038/s41467-020-17105-8). That bridge remains an inference: the positive carbachol data came from pancreatic MIN6 cells and heterologous systems, carbachol activates cholinergic receptors rather than binding NALCN directly, and neither the requirement for intact UNC80/UNC79 nor M3R-NALCN coupling has been tested in UNC80-deficient human neurons. A material null replication found muscarinic-agonist currents of similar frequency and amplitude in untransfected, M3R-expressing, and M3R/NALCN/UNC80/Src-expressing HEK293 cells, raising endogenous TRP conductance and weak membrane-glass seals as rival explanations for heterologous currents (DOI:10.1016/j.biopen.2018.01.001); even a genuine whole-cell response could remain spatially irrelevant where neuritic channel localization is lost. Translation is additionally constrained by exposure and selectivity: systemic carbachol behaved as a drug that did not cross an intact rat blood-brain barrier and changed behaviour only after focal barrier opening (DOI:10.1016/0024-3205(89)90289-0), while the marketed product is intraocular and its label records systemic parasympathomimetic adverse effects and no established paediatric safety (DAILYMED:f28d7a56-e079-4a8b-ad81-9a97c780de67). No exact-disease use or testing was identified. The critical experiment is therefore an allele-stratified carbachol concentration-response in patient-derived neurons with M3 blockade, NALCN knockdown or knockout, matched unedited controls, and compartment-resolved current and localization measurements; failure of a NALCN-specific response would favour loss of coupling, surface delivery, or neuritic localization over insufficient agonist drive.

## lithium carbonate

Lithium carbonate is a systemically deliverable source of Li+ that reaches human brain, with 0.52–0.87 mEq/L measured in ten lithium-carbonate-treated adults (PMCID:PMC8332749), but its proposed use here is a contingent phenotypic perturbation rather than a correction of the UNC80–NALCN channel defect. The positive basis is that 3 mM LiCl increased cell number and proliferation, but not survival, in a rodent neural-precursor line and primary rat and mouse precursors, with LiCl-versus-NaCl controls and pharmacological experiments implicating inhibitory GSK-3β phosphorylation and NF-AT activation (PMCID:PMC3125208). The directional bridge nevertheless assumes a lesion not yet shown in UNC80 deficiency: postnatal head-growth deceleration and microcephaly occur only in a subset, MRI is often structurally normal, and generalized short stature and low BMI make deficient brain-tissue accrual, skull growth, tissue loss, and systemic growth restriction live rival explanations (PMCID:PMC4716670; PMCID:PMC6671679; PMCID:PMC5671762). Translation is further limited because the proliferative experiment used LiCl at 3 mM rather than oral lithium carbonate at observed human brain exposure, a different murine neural-precursor line showed no growth difference at 0.1–1 mM Li2CO3 (PMID:9032938), and GSK-3 loss or inhibition suppresses SHH-driven postnatal cerebellar granule-neuron progenitor proliferation (PMCID:PMC6826032), making cell identity, region, developmental timing, and concentration plausible determinants of null or harmful effects. Exposure cannot simply be raised to reproduce the 3 mM culture result because the labelled toxic threshold of at least 1.5 mEq/L lies close to the 0.8–1.2 mEq/L therapeutic range (DAILYMED:57faf0b0-4380-4bbf-8de7-c3f9d74fc410). No published use or testing of lithium in UNC80 deficiency was located. The hypothesis is therefore testable only after an UNC80-deficient patient-derived progenitor or region-resolved organoid model demonstrates a baseline proliferation or tissue-accrual deficit; concentration-response studies should jointly measure EdU or BrdU incorporation, Ki-67, apoptosis, lineage output, organoid volume, cerebellar versus non-cerebellar effects, neuronal maturation and circuit function, and NALCN-dependent electrophysiology, with a null at clinically plausible brain concentrations, loss of appropriate differentiation or maturation, regional overgrowth, or a cerebellar decrement rejecting the proposed rescue.
